A score out of 100 based on research into how recruiters read CVs. It updates as you edit; click an item to jump to it.

Save as PDF (the yellow button) makes the CV you send to employers. Save draft downloads an editable .json file you can open here later and continue exactly where you left off.

Import: bring in an existing CV from a PDF (Word, Google Docs, CV websites or this editor) or pasted text. It becomes a new version with the sections filled in as well as the editor can tell; always check the result. A .json draft restores a CV exactly, an import is a best guess, so keep your drafts.

Everything stays on your computer. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Your work is also kept in this browser automatically, but use Save draft to keep a copy you can move, back up or reuse for another job.

Making the PDF: click Save as PDF / Print and choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. Set margins to None or Default and keep Background graphics on if your browser asks. Links stay clickable in the PDF.

Drag and drop: in the preview, grab a section, an entry or a single bullet and drop it where you want it. Sections can move between the two columns. Click anything in the preview to jump to it in the editor. Made a mistake? Undo or Ctrl+Z.

Review: a score out of 100 based on research into how recruiters read CVs, split into first glance, complete information, easy to scan, length, consistency and tailoring. Each tip shows how many points it's worth; click it to jump to the field. "Where the score comes from" at the bottom lists the research.

Backup: versions are stored in this browser only. Use Backup to download all of them in one file, and to restore them on another computer or browser.

Versions: keep one version per job with the dropdown at the top left. Duplicate copies the current version so you can tailor it, and Open… adds a saved file as a new version. Versions live in this browser, so use Save draft for backups.

Two pages: under Layout & style choose Two pages, then send sections to page 2 with the button on each section or by dragging them onto page 2.

Copy as text: gives a plain version for job portals that ask you to paste your CV.

Cover letter: switch to Cover letter at the top. It uses the same name, contact details, design and colours as your CV and is saved in the same file. Print it the same way.

Hide without deleting: use Hide on any section or entry to leave it off this version of your CV. It stays in your file so you can bring it back for another job.

Density: Compact tightens text and spacing slightly so more fits on the page.

Design: six looks to choose from. Each one works with every column layout, font style and colour.

Layout: sidebar left or right, one column, and three font styles. In the one-column layout, sidebar sections appear as a band under the profile.

Bold text: wrap words in double asterisks, for example **B driving licence**.

Top image: upload a certification badge, a logo or a photo, or use a text box instead (handy for "Exam scheduled" notes). For photos, pick a shape and drag the picture in the crop box to position it; the slider or mouse wheel zooms.

Recruiters scan the top left first. Put what matters most for the job there and at the top of each column.

One page: a red warning appears above the preview if your content no longer fits.

Job portals: automated application systems read one-column CVs most reliably. If a portal asks you to upload a CV, the one-column layout is the safest choice.

Keyword checker: only add a missing keyword if it's true for you. It's a guide, not a score to chase.
